# E98 King's Indian Defence: Orthodox, Aronin-Taimanov, 9. Ne1 <br> 1. d4 Nf6 2. c4 g6 3. Nc3 Bg7 4. e4 d6 5. Nf3 O-O 6. Be2 e5 7. O-O Nc6 8. d5 Ne7 9. Ne1 #

Continues from [E97's own "8... Ne7" node](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/E97_Kings_Indian_Mar_Del_Plata.md#_Ne7_), where **9. Ne1** (37.8% masters, narrowly behind the Bayonet Attack) reroutes the knight away from f3 toward d3 (supporting c5/b4 and covering the vulnerable e5-square) rather than launching the pawns immediately. This is the classical, patient approach to the Mar del Plata — Boris Spassky and, later, Garry Kasparov both handled this exact plan from the White side in some of the King's Indian's most celebrated games. Live-tagged **King's Indian Defense: Orthodox Variation, Classical System** — a name `eco.md` doesn't carry at all (its own entry is simply "Aronin-Taimanov, 9.Ne1").

**9... Nd7** (86.3% masters) rerouts Black's own knight from f6, clearing the way for ... f5 without allowing White's own knight to hop into an awkward square along the way, and is the clear masters' favourite — well ahead of the online-competitive but comparatively rare **9... Ne8** (37.1% online, only 12.1% masters), the same rerouting idea via the back rank instead. Both aim at the same eventual ... f5 break; Nd7 is simply considered more accurate today.
