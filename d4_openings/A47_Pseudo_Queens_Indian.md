# A47 Pseudo Queen's Indian Defense <br> 1. d4 Nf6 2. Nf3 b6 #

Spun off from [A40's 1... Nf6 2. Nf3 note](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/A40_QPG.md#_Nf6_Nf3_), which had no candidate bullet or card pointing to it — a genuine zero-coverage gap surfaced by a full ECO-code audit rather than a shallow mention anywhere in the repo. The name is Lichess's own: it looks like a Queen's Indian fianchetto (... b6 preparing ... Bb7), but without an early c4 from White, Black isn't actually pressuring c4 the way the real Queen's Indian does — hence "Pseudo."

> [!NOTE]
> A genuinely flat spread with no dominant try — **3. g3** (28.2% masters) is only a whisker ahead of **3. c4** (27.0%), with **3. Bg5** (17.3%) and **3. Bf4** (14.4%) both real too. Committing c4 immediately transposes straight into the true Queen's Indian once Black plays ... Bb7 — the "Pseudo" label really only describes the moves before White's own c4 arrives.

### Candidate moves

* **3. g3** (+0.3, 28.2% masters): the line this card follows — fianchettoes to match Black's own setup.
* **3. c4** (+0.2, 27.0% masters): transposes directly into the real [Queen's Indian Defense](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/E12_Queens_Indian.md) once Black continues ... Bb7.
* **3. Bg5** (+0.2, 17.3% masters): pins nothing yet (the f6-knight isn't defending anything) but develops actively before Black's own bishop gets out.
* **3. Bf4** (+0.1, 14.4% masters): develops outside the pawn chain, similar in spirit to the London System's own bishop placement.
