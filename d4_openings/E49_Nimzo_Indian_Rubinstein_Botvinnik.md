# E49 Nimzo-Indian Defense: Normal Variation, Botvinnik System <br> 1. d4 Nf6 2. c4 e6 3. Nc3 Bb4 4. e3 O-O 5. Bd3 d5 6. a3 Bxc3 7. bxc3 #

Spun off from [E48's own root fork](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/E48_Nimzo_Indian_Rubinstein_Classical.md): a real second choice at White's 6th move (19.4% masters), forcing the bishop trade immediately to build the same doubled-pawn structure the Sämisch Variation reaches, but a full tempo down (since Black has already castled and White has already developed the bishop to d3). Live-tagged the *Botvinnik System* — the natural end of the whole E40-E49 Rubinstein System sweep.

**7... dxc4** is masters' overwhelming reply (76.0%), grabbing the pawn immediately since White's own bishop just moved off the a6-f1 diagonal and can't easily recapture it favourably. Deeper Botvinnik System theory past this point is its own extensive body of work, not covered further here — this is the natural end of the whole E40-E49 Rubinstein System sweep as covered across this card set.
