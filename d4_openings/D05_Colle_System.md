# D05 Queen's Pawn Game: Colle System <br> 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 e6 #

Split off from [D04_Colle_System.md](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/D04_Colle_System.md) on 2026-08-25, after a stage-2 pass of this repo's own Phase D ECO audit found the split was overdue: the whole card had been built around **3... e6** (masters' top pick at 32.6%), but that exact position is live-tagged **D05**, not D04 — the mermaid's own tooltips already said so when the card was first written, they just never got acted on. D04 itself stays the correct code for every *other* 3rd-move reply (c5, c6, Bf5, g6, Bg4), which is why they're back on `D04_Colle_System.md` instead of here. Named after Belgian master Edgar Colle: rather than fight for a specific structure, White commits to a fixed plan — e3, Bd3, Nbd2, O-O, and eventually the thematic e3-e4 break — that plays almost the same way regardless of what Black does.

## 4... c5 — the Colle tabiya

Here masters actually prefer **5. b3** (63.6%) — heading for the hybrid setup with Bb2 aiming at the long diagonal, which the explorer tags **Rubinstein Opening** rather than the "Colle-Zukertort" name books often use — over the traditional **5. c3** (23.4%), the *Colle System, Traditional Colle* structure. Either way White simply continues Nbd2, O-O and the thematic e3-e4 break in more or less any order, since the whole appeal of a system opening is not depending too heavily on move order or Black's exact setup.
