# A45 Trompowsky Attack <br> 1. d4 Nf6 2. Bg5 #

Spun off from [A40's 1... Nf6 stats table](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/d4_openings/A40_QPG.md#_Nf6_), where Bg5 already sat in the numbers (3.7% masters, 4.5% online) with no candidate bullet or card pointing to it — a genuine silent gap, not just a shallow mention. Named after English IM Julian Hodgson's championing of the move in the 1980s-90s, the Trompowsky pins nothing (Black's knight isn't defending anything on f6 yet) but immediately puts the question to Black's own next move, sidestepping the vast Indian Defense theory that follows 2. c4/2. Nf3 entirely.

> [!NOTE]
> A genuine three-way fork with no dominant try at master level — **2... Ne4** (29.3%), **2... d5** (25.8%), and **2... e6** (24.5%) are all within five points of each other. Online, **2... e6** edges ahead instead (27.5%), with **2... Ne4** a close second (23.4%).

### Candidate moves

* **2... Ne4** (0.0, 29.3% masters): immediately challenges the bishop, gaining a tempo — the line this card follows.
* **2... d5** (0.0, 25.8% masters): a solid, Queen's-Gambit-flavoured structure, ignoring the bishop's pin-adjacent placement.
* **2... e6** (0.0, 24.5% masters): keeps the knight on f6, preparing ... h6 to question the bishop on Black's own terms next.
* **2... c5** (+0.1, 12.1% masters): strikes at the centre immediately, accepting doubled pawns after a likely Bxf6 in some lines.
