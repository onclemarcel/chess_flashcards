# B21 Sicilian Defense: McDonnell Attack / Smith-Morra Gambit <br> 1. e4 c5 2. f4 (or 2. d4) #

Spun off from [B20's own root fork](https://github.com/onclemarcel/chess_flashcards/blob/main/e4_openings/B20_Sicilian.md): two genuinely different 2nd-move tries share this one code — **2. f4**, live-tagged the *McDonnell Attack* (`eco.md` calls it the "Grand Prix Attack," a real name divergence — Lichess reserves that name for the same idea reached one move later, via 2. Nc3 Nc6 3. f4), and **2. d4**, the *Smith-Morra Gambit*, offering the d-pawn back immediately for rapid development and open lines. Both live-confirmed B21 via the Lichess explorer's own `opening` field.

**2... d5!** is masters' top try (37.4%), hitting back in the centre before White's kingside plan gets moving: after **3. exd5 Nf6**, Black regains the pawn with a comfortable game, since the knight both attacks d5 and prepares to meet **4. Nc3** with ... Nxd5. This is why the McDonnell Attack scores much better online (4.4% of tries) than in masters practice (0.4%) — it rewards an opponent who doesn't know the simple central answer.
